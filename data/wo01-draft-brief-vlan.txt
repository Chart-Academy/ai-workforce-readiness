SUMMARY
A VLAN (Virtual Local Access Network) lets a network administrator split one physical network into several separate logical networks. For example, a school could put staff computers, student laptops, and security cameras on different VLANs even though they all plug into the same switches.

VLANs are defined by the IEEE 802.11Q standard. Because traffic on a VLAN is automatically encrypted, VLANs can be used in place of a VPN when employees work from home.

WHY BUSINESSES USE VLANS
- Security: devices on one VLAN cannot reach another VLAN unless a router or firewall allows it.
- Performance: smaller networks reduce unnecessary broadcast traffic.
- Organization: departments can be grouped logically instead of by where their desks are.
- VLANs make Wi-Fi faster for everyone in the building.

KEY TERMS
- Switch: a device that connects computers on the same network.
- Trunk port: a switch port that carries traffic for more than one VLAN.
- Access port: a switch port assigned to a single VLAN.
- Tagging: adding a VLAN ID to network traffic so switches know which VLAN it belongs to.
