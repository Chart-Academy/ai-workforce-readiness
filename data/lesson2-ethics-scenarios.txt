LESSON 2 PRACTICE: Workplace AI Ethics Scenarios
Practice data for AI for Workforce Readiness. All people and companies are made up.

For each scenario, decide: Is this OK, not OK, or "it depends"? What should the person do instead?

1. TANYA, medical receptionist
   Tanya pastes a patient's name, date of birth, and diagnosis into a free AI chatbot to help her write a letter to the insurance company.

2. MARCUS, custodian
   Marcus asks an AI assistant whether it's safe to mix bleach and glass cleaner to make a "stronger" cleaner. The AI says yes. He tries it.

3. DENISE, job seeker
   Denise asks AI to rewrite her résumé. It adds "Managed a team of 25 employees." She actually scheduled 25 volunteers by group text. She leaves it in because it "sounds better."

4. JORDAN, warehouse associate
   Jordan's company has a written policy: no AI tools on company computers. Jordan uses AI on a personal phone to write emails to customers, including customer order numbers.

5. LUIS, network technician
   Luis uses AI to write a report for his supervisor about a network outage. He turns it in without reading it closely. It names the wrong server as the cause.

6. KEISHA, hiring manager
   Keisha uses an AI tool to screen 200 applications. It ranks people lower if they have a gap in their work history. Many of those applicants were caregivers.

7. AMIR, student in this class
   Amir uses AI to answer the "In your own words" reflection for a lab and submits it in Canvas as his own writing.

8. ROSA, office assistant
   Rosa uses AI to draft a reply to an upset customer, then edits it to sound like her, checks the facts, and sends it. She tells her supervisor she uses AI for first drafts.
