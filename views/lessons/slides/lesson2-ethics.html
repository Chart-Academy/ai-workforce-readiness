Ethics

Using AI in ways that protect you, your coworkers, and your employer.

Learning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Identify personal and workplace information that should never be entered into an AI tool.
  2. Describe how bias can show up in hiring tools and AI answers, and name one way to reduce it.
  3. Decide whether a workplace use of AI is OK, not OK, or “it depends,” and explain why.
  4. Write a personal rule for using AI honestly at work.

Privacy

What never goes into an AI tool

  • Identity numbers

    Social Security, driver's license, passport

  • Money

    Bank accounts, card numbers, pay stubs

  • Passwords

    Logins, door codes, security answers

  • Health

    Medical records, diagnoses, medications

  • Other people's details

    Customer and coworker names, phones, addresses

  • Company secrets

    Internal files, prices, network maps

Swap real details for placeholders: “Customer A,” “[address],” “[phone].” The AI still helps, and nothing private leaves your hands.

Bias

Unfair in, unfair out

  1. Past examplesMost past hires came from one group
  2. Learned pattern“People like them are good hires”
  3. Unfair resultA résumé screener ranks other people lower
  • Ask who might be left out of an answer
  • Ask the AI for other points of view
  • A person, not the tool, makes decisions about people

Company AI policies

Know the rules before you use AI at work

  1. ReadFind your company's AI policy
  2. Use approved toolsNot every AI app is allowed
  3. Ask firstUnsure? Check with your supervisor
  4. Be openSay when AI helped, if the rules ask

Good questions for your supervisor

  • Which AI tools are we allowed to use?
  • What information can I put into them?
  • Do I need to say when AI helped with my work?

Honesty

AI-assisted, or passing it off?

  • AI-assisted (OK)

    • The ideas and facts come from you
    • You check everything it wrote
    • You edit it to sound like you
    • You say AI helped when asked

  • Passing it off (not OK)

    • Copy, paste, submit without reading
    • Claim skills or experience you don't have
    • Hide AI use when the rules say to share it

Before you hit enter

Three questions to ask yourself

  1. Is anything private in here?Remove it or use a placeholder
  2. Is this allowed here?Approved tool, approved task
  3. Would I explain it to my boss?If not, don't do it

Not OK

Maria pastes a customer's complaint, with their name and phone number, into a free AI app.

Better

Maria removes the name and number, writes “[customer],” and checks that her store allows the tool.
