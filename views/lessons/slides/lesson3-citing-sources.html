Accountability: Citing Sources

You are responsible for anything you hand in, even if AI helped write it.

Learning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Locate and open the original source behind an AI answer.
  2. Identify made-up facts, quotes, and links in an AI-written draft.
  3. Cite sources in a simple, consistent format.
  4. Write a short note explaining where and how AI was used in your work.

The big idea

Your name is on it

  1. AI drafts itFast, but it can be wrong
  2. You check itEvery fact, name, number, and link
  3. You own itYour boss sees your name, not the AI's

“The AI said so” is never an excuse. If it's wrong when you hand it in, it's your mistake.

Show your work

Ask for sources, then open them

  1. Ask“List your sources with links.”
  2. OpenClick every link yourself
  3. FindIs the fact really on that page?
  4. Keep or cutNo proof? Fix it or take it out.

A source you haven't opened is not a source.

Red flags

Spotting made-up facts, quotes, and links

  • Dead or wrong links

    The page doesn't exist, or it's about something else.

  • Perfect quotes

    A great line from a famous person you can't find anywhere else.

  • Exact numbers, no source

    “73% of workers…” Where did that number come from?

  • Reports you can't find

    A study or law with a real-sounding name that a search doesn't turn up.

Give credit

A simple citation format

  1. WhoPerson or organization
  2. TitleName of the page or report
  3. Where & whenWebsite, and the date you read it
  4. LinkSo anyone can check it

Example

Federal Trade Commission. “How to Recognize and Avoid Phishing Scams.” Consumer Advice, accessed October 14, 2026. consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-avoid-phishing-scams

Be upfront

Noting where and how you used AI

Say

  • Which tool
  • What it did
  • What you checked or changed

Example AI note

“I used ChatGPT to make a first draft of this summary and to suggest sources. I opened every source, fixed two wrong dates, and wrote the conclusion myself.”

Put it at the end of the document, or wherever your teacher or employer asks.
