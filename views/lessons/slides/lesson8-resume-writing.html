Résumé Writing

Strengthen the résumé you already have, or build your first one.

Real-world problem

Your résumé, stronger

  • Out of date

    Your last job isn't on it, or the dates are old

  • Duties, not results

    “Responsible for…” instead of what you got done

  • A gap

    Time away from work with no explanation

  • No résumé yet

    You'll build one from the starter worksheet

Hiring managers skim. Your job is to make the right things easy to find, and true.

Privacy first

Before you paste it into AI

Don't share

Jordan Lee 412 Elm St, Apt 3, Springfield (555) 014-8812 · jlee88@example.com

Share this instead

Jordan Lee [Address] [Phone] · [Email]

  • Work on a copy. Keep your original file safe
  • Never include your Social Security number, birth date, or references' contact details

Pick a layout

Choose a format that fits your story

  • Work history first

    Steady work in one field. Most recent job at the top.

  • Skills first

    Changing fields, a long gap, or a first job. Skills lead, jobs follow.

  • A mix of both

    A short skills section on top, then your work history.

The core skill

Write bullet points that show results

  1. ActionStart with a strong verb: led, trained, fixed, served
  2. TaskWhat you did
  3. ResultWhat changed because of you
  4. NumberHow many, how often, how much, if you know it

Weak

“Responsible for the food pantry.”

Strong

“Organized a weekly church food pantry, serving 60+ families with a team of 8 volunteers.”

Get feedback

Let AI review it. You make the changes.

Ask, with the checklist

“Review my résumé using this checklist. For each item, tell me what's working and one way to make it stronger. Don't rewrite it for me.”

Then:

  • Pick the 3 changes that matter most
  • Make them yourself, in your words
  • Every number and fact must be true

AI can't know what you did. If it adds a skill or number you can't back up, take it out.

Time away from work

Explaining a gap

Leave it blank

2019 – 2025: nothing listed

One honest line

Family Caregiver, 2019 – 2025
Managed medications, appointments, and a household budget for a parent with a long-term illness.

  • Caregiving, school, health, moving, or looking for work are all fine reasons
  • Keep it short and honest. Save the details for the interview

Aim it at one job

Tailor a copy to a real posting

  1. Read the postingHighlight the skills they mention most
  2. Match, honestlyUse their words where they're true for you
  3. Keep it simpleNo text boxes, pictures, or contact info in the header, so screening software can read it

Save it with a clear name: Firstname-Lastname-Resume-Warehouse.docx
