Module 1 · Foundations · Lesson 1

Introduction to Artificial Intelligence (AI)

What AI is, how chat assistants answer, and where AI is already at work.

Learning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Explain the difference between chat assistants, agents, and automation, and give one workplace example of each.
  2. Write a prompt that includes a role, a task, context, and a format.
  3. Identify at least two ways an AI answer can be wrong, and explain why each happens.
  4. Compare the answers to a weak prompt and a strong prompt, and describe what improved.

The big idea

What is AI?

Software that learns patterns from huge numbers of examples, then uses those patterns to make a guess.

  1. ExamplesMillions of photos, emails, web pages, and books
  2. PatternsIt learns what usually goes with what
  3. A guessIt predicts the most likely answer for something new

Saw thousands of cat photos → spots a cat in a new photo.
Read billions of sentences → writes a likely next sentence.

Under the hood

How a chat assistant answers

  1. It reads your prompt
  2. It guesses the most likely next word
  3. It repeats, word by word, until the answer is done

It picks what sounds likely. It does not check what is true.

“Our store opens at ”

  • 9 am58%
  • 8 am27%
  • 10 am11%
  • noon4%

Example numbers. It doesn't know your store's hours unless you tell it.

Three kinds of AI tools

Chat assistant, agent, or automation?

  • Chat assistant

    You ask, it answers. One back-and-forth at a time.

    “Help me reword this email.”

    Next step decided by: you

  • Agent

    An assistant with a job description and tools. It works through several steps toward a goal.

    “Find 5 job openings near me and list them in a table.”

    Next step decided by: the AI, inside your rules

  • Automation

    The same fixed steps every time. No judgment, no surprises.

    “Text me when a timesheet is missing.”

    Next step decided by: pre-set rules

Already on the job

Where AI is used at work today

  • Help desk & Information Technology (IT)

    Chatbots answer common questions first

  • Warehouse

    Predicts stock needs and plans routes

  • Healthcare

    Drafts visit notes and books appointments

  • Retail

    Answers customer chats and tracks orders

  • Office & admin

    Drafts emails and summarizes meetings

  • Hiring

    Screens résumés (more on bias in Lesson 2)

The skill for today

Writing a clear prompt

  1. RoleWho should the AI act as?
  2. TaskWhat exactly should it do?
  3. ContextWhat does it need to know?
  4. FormatHow should the answer look?

Weak

“Write an email about being late.”

Strong

“You are a professional office assistant. Write an email to my supervisor saying I will be 20 minutes late today. My bus broke down, my shift starts at 8 am, and I have never been late before. Keep it to 3 sentences with a clear subject line.”

Know the limits

Where AI gets things wrong, and why

  • Makes things up

    Invents facts, quotes, and links that sound real, because it fills gaps with likely words.

  • Out of date

    It learned from data up to a cutoff date. Newer news, prices, and rules can be missing.

  • Missing your context

    It doesn't know your job, your customer, or your schedule unless you tell it.

  • Bias

    It can repeat unfair patterns that were in its examples.

Sounding confident is not the same as being correct. You are responsible for checking.
