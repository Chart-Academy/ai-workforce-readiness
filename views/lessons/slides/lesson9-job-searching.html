Job Searching

Find real openings that fit your skills, schedule, and commute, and keep your search organized.

Learning objectives

The learner will be able to:

  1. Name at least three places to find real job openings, including local workforce centers.
  2. Use filters, keywords, and job alerts to narrow a search to jobs that fit your pay, schedule, and commute.
  3. Identify the red flags of a fake listing or job scam.
  4. Track applications in Google Sheets and write a polite follow-up email.

Real-world problem

25 applications. No replies. No record.

What Marcus did

  • Applied to anything, anywhere
  • Didn't check bus times or shifts
  • Didn't write down where
  • Never followed up

What works better

  • Decide your must-haves first
  • Apply to fewer, better-fitting jobs
  • Track every application
  • Follow up after about a week

Start here

Must-haves and nice-to-haves

Must-haves (deal breakers)

  • Weekdays only, between childcare drop-off and pick-up
  • 30 minutes or less by bus
  • At least $17 an hour
  • Starts by mid-November

Nice-to-haves

  • Health benefits
  • Room to move up
  • Close to daycare

A job that misses one must-have isn't a fit, no matter how good the pay looks.

Where to look

Five places to find openings

  • Job sites

    Big job boards with filters and alerts

  • Company websites

    The careers page is the most reliable source

  • Staffing agencies

    Temp and temp-to-hire jobs. They never charge you a fee.

  • Workforce centers

    Free résumé help, hiring events, and local openings

  • People you know

    Friends, family, and past coworkers hear about jobs first

Search smart

Let the tools do the sorting

  1. KeywordsJob titles and skills: “inventory clerk,” “forklift”
  2. FiltersPay, distance, schedule, date posted
  3. AlertsGet new matches by email every day
  4. VerifyFind the same job on the employer's own site

Ask AI

“I have 3 years of warehouse picking and inventory experience and a forklift certificate. List 10 job titles I should search for, and the keywords employers use for each.”

Protect yourself

Spotting job scams

  • You pay first

    Starter kits, training fees, or equipment you buy “now”

  • Private info too early

    Social Security or bank numbers before an interview or offer

  • Chat-only hiring

    No interview, or only by text or a chat app

  • Too good to be true

    High pay, no experience, work from home, start today

  • No real company

    No website, a personal email, or no address

Real employers never ask you to pay to get a job.

Follow up

A short, polite follow-up email

Example

Subject: Following up on my Inventory Clerk application

Hello Ms. Green,

I applied for the Inventory Clerk position on October 14 and wanted to check on the status. I have three years of inventory experience and would welcome the chance to talk. Thank you for your time.

Marcus Hill
[Phone]

Wait about a week after applying. One follow-up is enough.
