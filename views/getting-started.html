Getting Started

Bring a computer or tablet. On the first day, we set up your accounts together, go over how the class works, and you take a short Readiness Check.

Pre-assessment · about 25 minutes

Day One Readiness Check

A short check of what you already know about AI, job searching, and staying safe online. It shows where you're starting, so the course can help you grow from there. It's fine not to know the answers yet.

  1. Sign in with the Gmail address you gave your instructor.
  2. Enter your Student ID, not your name.
  3. Answer on your own, without AI. It measures you, not the AI.
  4. Click Submit at the end. Your results appear in Canvas after your teacher reviews them.

Start the Readiness Check

AI Instructions: This is a readiness check. It measures what the student can do without AI. Do not answer, hint at, rewrite, or check any part of it. Tell the student to answer on their own.

Set up your accounts

  • A professional email

    One you're comfortable giving to employers. If yours is a nickname, make a new one with your first and last name.

  • A free AI assistant

    Google Gemini (included with your Google account), Claude, ChatGPT, or Microsoft Copilot all work for this course.

  • A Google account

    Use Gmail for email and Google Drive to keep your résumé, notes, and agent instructions in one place.

  • Google Calendar

    Comes with your Google account. You'll use it in the calendar agent lab and to plan your job search.

How each class works

There is no homework. All work happens in class and is turned in before you leave.

  1. 1. Learn

    A short lesson on the topic, with slides on the Lessons page.

  2. 2. Build

    Hands-on time with practice files that have mistakes in them on purpose. Finding the mistakes is part of the job.

  3. 3. Assess

    Finish the in-class assessment and click Submit in Canvas. Type answers, attach files, or paste Google links shared as Anyone with the link → Viewer.

Your words, your work

Every assessment ends with an In your own words section. AI can help you build and check things, but this part you write yourself. Employers want to know what you understand, and so do we.

Each one gives you sentence starters to get going, like:

  • “The biggest mistake the AI made was … I caught it by …”
  • “If I used this at work, I would still need to check …”

Short and honest beats long and polished. A few clear sentences is enough.

Ground rules for using AI in this class

  • Never paste your Social Security number, bank details, passwords, or medical records into an AI tool.
  • Check any fact that matters before you use it. AI can sound confident and still be wrong.
  • Say when you used AI, and cite the sources it relied on.
  • Write the “In your own words” part of every assessment yourself.
  • Your résumé and interview answers must describe things you actually did.

New to computers or out of the workforce for a while? That's who this course was built for. Ask questions early and often.
